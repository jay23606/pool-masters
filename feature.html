Pool Masters · Roadmap

What we have built,
and what is next.

One roadmap. It compares the game with five commercial pool titles, lists what has shipped, ranks what is worth building next, and names the things we are deliberately not copying.

Compiled September 2026 from each game's own store listing, so it shows what they advertise, not everything they do. Where a listing does not mention a feature the table says ? (unknown), never "no". See Sources. Generated by tools/build-roadmap.mjs.

01The field

Five commercial games, chosen to span the market: the mobile giant, a free-to-play PC title, and three paid PC games from a career-focused simulator to a 27-game collection.

Pool Masters

This project. Free in any browser, installable, with no ads and no purchases. Live voice and video in the match, a host-authoritative simulation, a shot coach, replay links, a daily shot, a career, challenges, house rules, and game modes nobody else lists.

8 Ball Pool

Miniclip. Free with in-app purchases; 4.8 million ratings on the App Store. 8-ball and 9-ball, ranked leagues that unlock venues, clubs and club quests, collectible cues, tables and avatars, friend challenges, spectating, controlled chat, and aim guidelines.

Pure Pool Pro

VooFoo Studios. $24.99 on PC. Career in 8-ball and 9-ball with handcrafted opponents across hundreds of events, challenge games, a daily challenge, leaderboards, crossplay online and local multiplayer, and unlockable cues, ball sets and baize.

Virtual Pool 4

Celeris. $24.99 on PC, with a mobile version. 27 games including snooker, billiards and straight pool, two career modes, single and double elimination tournaments, a trick-shot library, and video tutorials.

Cue Club 2

$18.99 on PC. Pool and snooker: 6, 7, 8, 9, 10-ball, 14.1, speed pool, killer and snooker, customisable rules, 2 to 4 players and spectating, bars to unlock, tables from 6 to 12 ft, and slow-motion replay with save.

Real Pool 3D (Poolians)

Free with in-app purchases on Steam. 8-ball, 15-ball, snooker and coin-team games, teams of up to six, more than ten prized events a day, avatars, profiles, chat and a friends list.

02Shipped (22)

Everything below is live. Each card says what it does and, where it matters, what it does not do yet.

✓

A second game mode — 9-ball

Shipped. Ten-ball diamond rack, lowest-ball-first, the cushion rule, 9-on-a-legal-shot wins (combinations included), 9 re-spotted on a foul, ball in hand after any foul. It reused the whole physics/rendering/networking stack — this was a new rules.js variant and a smaller rack, exactly as scoped. Available in practice, private tables and quick play; unranked until the ranking tables know which game a result came from.

Shipped
✓

Replay links

Shipped. Every shot is recorded as it plays — by the host from its simulation, by guests and spectators from the snapshots they receive — and Copy replay link packs it into a URL. A full 16-ball break is about 1,000 characters. Anyone can open the link and watch it (at full or quarter speed), with sound. It records positions rather than re-simulating inputs, on purpose: two JavaScript engines needn't agree to the last bit on sin, cos or pow, and billiards amplifies a last-bit difference into a different shot.

Shipped
✓

Trick-shot / practice drill library

Shipped. Eleven one-shot drills on fixed tables, from a straight-in pot to draw, follow, stop, bank, combination and kick shots, with a level, a tip, a Show me that dials in the aim, power and spin, and progress kept in the browser. A solve the hint aimed completes the drill but sets no record. Every drill is proven solvable: a solver searched the real physics for each one, and the tests run its hint through the game again, so a physics change that breaks a drill fails the build. It also caught that the break tip was wrong (full power with no spin scratches every time).

Shipped
✓

Achievements / trophies

Shipped. Twenty-seven trophies in seven groups (racks, nine-ball, skill, opponents, practice, social, league), each with a progress bar toward its target, a toast when one unlocks and a trophy case in the header. Stats are derived from what happened on the table, so host and guest agree, and kept per device. Building it also found and fixed a real bug: a shot that ended before the host's first snapshot was invisible to a guest.

Shipped
✓

Physics-constant inspector

Shipped, read-only. TABLE → How the physics works lists every constant the simulation runs on (friction, restitution, spin decay, rest threshold, the fixed step), what each does, the power-to-speed curve in game units and m/s, and ball and pocket size per table. Values are read live from physics.js, and a test fails if a constant is added there without being described, so the panel cannot drift. It is deliberately not tweakable: the same numbers drive every game, so every player sees the same shot.

Shipped
✓

Daily shot

Shipped. One fixed table a day, the same for everyone, that a search of the real physics has shown can be solved. Difficulty rises through the week, the result is a shareable line, and there is no account or server, so it works offline. Two trophies hang off it.

Shipped
✓

Shot coach

Shipped. After a shot, the 🎓 Coach grades it against the best the AI can find from the same table: what you potted or why it was a foul, how often a shot like yours works for a steady player, and the coach's own pick with its odds, both watchable as replays on the table as it stood. It is the AI's play-it-out check turned on a person, and its answer is reproducible for a given table.

Shipped
✓

Hot-seat: two players, one device

Shipped. Pass the phone: no room code, no account, no network, and it works offline. It is the practice game with a second person where the AI was, so it plays both 8-ball and 9-ball with ball in hand, races to three, and names the players throughout. Ranked results and stats are deliberately left out.

Shipped
✓

Bank pool and one-pocket

Shipped. Two more games on a full rack of fifteen, first to eight. In bank pool a ball only scores if it touched a cushion on its way in; in one-pocket each player owns a foot corner and a ball scores for whoever owns the pocket it dropped in. Both are one pure rules function, the AI plays both (it finds bank shots by mirroring the pockets and confirming each candidate with a real play-out), and the shot coach knows what counts.

Shipped
✓

Cosmetics earned, not bought

Shipped. Trophies unlock four cues, four felts and three rail woods; locked choices show the trophy that unlocks them, and the trophy case says what each one gives. Everything that was free stays free, and the game still has no ads or purchases.

Shipped
✓

Career mode

Shipped. A ladder of ten opponents and venues across all four games that opens up as you win, on seven rungs of AI from novice to legend, with trophies and cosmetics as rewards. No server needed.

Shipped
✓

Challenge games

Shipped. Speed Pot, Perfect Potter and Clear the Table, each with a best.

Shipped
✓

10-ball and straight pool

Shipped. Ten-ball on the nine-ball rules; straight pool as a race to 30 with re-racks and a point off a foul (uncalled).

Shipped
✓

House rules

Shipped. Race length, what the cue ball does after a foul, who breaks, the straight-pool score, and jump shots on or off. Online tables carry the rules and are never ranked when they are not the defaults.

Shipped
✓

Jump shots

Shipped. An optional house rule: a Jump button hops the cue ball over a ball in its way. In 2D the ball swells while it is airborne; in 3D it really rises.

Shipped
✓

Aim snapping and a follow-the-shot camera

Shipped. Aim snaps to the middle of a pocket (or a one-cushion bank) when you are close, can be turned off, and the 3D camera can follow the ball with an adjustable eye height.

Shipped
✓

Rogue Pool

Shipped. A solo roguelike run: clear each table within its shots, take one of three random upgrades (wider pockets, extra shots, lives, jump charges, a scratch shield, a second wind), and lose a life when you run out. The same seed is the same run.

Shipped
✓

Chaos Pool

Shipped. A scored game to 15 with a random twist dealt before every shot: a bonus pocket worth three, a bomb ball that blasts its neighbours, or a gravity well that bends the balls. The host deals it, so both players see the same one.

Shipped
✓

A tidier lobby and a first-run tutorial

Shipped. The lobby is grouped: a primary row (Find a game, Practice vs AI, Two players on one device), the game settings (game, AI, table, house rules), then Sharpen your game (How to play, Daily shot, Drills and trick shots, Challenges, Puzzle maker) and Solo runs (Career, Rogue Pool). A new player is offered a five-step tutorial once: aim, power, cutting, spin and banks, each an ordinary drill with a plain explanation and a Show me.

Shipped
✓

Curated trick shots

Shipped. Twenty tables that one shot clears completely (eight with two balls, eight with three, four with four), under Drills. Each has a Show me button that sets up and plays the proven shot. Every one was found by searching the real physics, and the tests replay each through the physics, so a change that breaks one fails the build.

Shipped
✓

Obstacle tables

Shipped. A table option in the lobby for practice and two players on one device: Bumpers, Walls, Portals or the Gauntlet. Balls bounce off bumpers and walls and come out of the paired portal going the same way; a jump shot hops a bumper or wall. The AI, the solver and the physics all see them. Not yet: online tables, and placing your own in the puzzle maker.

Shipped
✓

Puzzle maker

Shipped. Arrange a table, pick the ball to pot, and the computer searches the real physics for a shot. Only proven-solvable tables can be shared, and a puzzle link re-proves itself when it is opened.

Shipped

03What is worth building next

Ranked by how many of the five have it and how well it fits what the game already is. Each item names who has it.

1

Tournaments and brackets

Four of the five have tournaments, and it is the feature that gives a player a reason to come back tomorrow. Virtual Pool 4 offers single and double elimination, with handicaps; Real Pool 3D runs ten prized events a day. We have a race-to-3 match and nothing above it. The bracket logic is pure and easy; the cost is that a tournament needs shared, durable state, which means new database tables.

Seen in: 8 Ball Pool, Virtual Pool 4, Cue Club 2, Real Pool 3D

High impact
2

Killer and more than two players

Cue Club 2 seats up to four and lists killer; Real Pool 3D has teams of up to six. Hot-seat for three or four players, killer/cut-throat, and two-versus-two are cheap to add to the shared-device mode. More than two online players changes the host-authoritative model and is a large piece of work.

Seen in: Cue Club 2, Real Pool 3D

Next
3

Ghost race

Play against a friend’s recorded run of a drill, daily shot or Rogue table, shown as a ghost cue ball. Replays already record positions, so this needs no server: the run travels in a link.

Seen in: Nobody listed

Next
4

Friends list and clubs

8 Ball Pool, Pure Pool, Virtual Pool 4 and Real Pool 3D have friends; 8 Ball Pool adds clubs and club quests. Deferred for now, since both need new database tables and it is not needed yet.

Seen in: 8 Ball Pool, Pure Pool Pro, Virtual Pool 4, Real Pool 3D

Deferred
5

Replay director and accessibility

Replays record positions, so they can be played from any camera: scrub, slow-mo through the contact, orbit the cue ball. Separately, colour-blind-safe ball patterns, spoken shot commentary and larger touch targets, since every game here relies on colour alone to tell solids from stripes.

Seen in: Nobody listed

Lower priority
6

Called shots in straight pool, and Checkpoint or Rumble

Straight pool here is uncalled, unlike the real game. Pure Pool also has Checkpoint and Royal Rumble challenge modes we do not.

Seen in: Pure Pool Pro, Cue Club 2

Lower priority
7

Snooker and carom

Three of the five have snooker or carom billiards. It means a bigger table, different balls and scoring, and a different physics tuning. A large piece of work for a different audience, so it stays at the bottom.

Seen in: Virtual Pool 4, Cue Club 2, Real Pool 3D

Lower priority

04What we are deliberately not copying

Commercial games have things that make money, not things that make a better game. Naming them keeps the roadmap honest.

✕

A coin economy, loot boxes and a paid pass

8 Ball Pool sells coins, a Pool Pass and random items. Pool Masters has no ads and no purchases, and that is worth more to a player than another currency. Cosmetics stay earned.

Not planned
✕

Perks from owning more cues

8 Ball Pool grants power for a bigger cue collection, which rewards spending. Cosmetics here are only cosmetic.

Not planned
✕

VR

Real Pool 3D lists it as a tag. It would need a headset-first rebuild for a very small audience.

Not planned
✕

A native mobile app wrapper

Four of the five competitors are mobile-native. The installable PWA already works offline for practice, and wrapping it for the app stores is ongoing work (review, push, updates) for no new game. The PWA is the mobile app.

Not planned
