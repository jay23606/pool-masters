Pool Masters · Competitive Landscape

Where we stand,
and where to go next.

A feature-by-feature comparison of Pool Masters against five other pool/billiards games — two open source, three closed source — built to turn "what should we build next" into an evidence-based list rather than a guess.

Compiled September 2026 from each project's own documentation, store listings and public repositories. Closed-source entries are necessarily based on what's publicly visible — see Sources at the end.

01The field

Six pool games, chosen to cover the range: a technically serious open-source physics sim, a research-grade simulator, the mass-market mobile leader, a 3D/AR-forward mobile competitor, and a feature-rich desktop PC title.

Pool Masters

This project. Browser-based 8-ball with real-time voice/video, host-authoritative netcode with client-side prediction, and a from-scratch physics engine (angular velocity, skid-to-roll, throw, spin-aware cushions).

tailuge/billiards

Open source. In-browser 8/9-ball, snooker and 3-cushion carom with a serious physics core, replay links, and tools to inspect and tweak the physics itself.

Pooltool

Open source. A Python/Panda3D billiards simulator built for science and engineering as much as play — event-based analytical physics, JIT-compiled, with a documented API for shot analysis.

8 Ball Pool (Miniclip)

Closed source. The market leader by scale: coin economy, tournaments, league tiers, cue cosmetics, and cross-platform play including Messenger/iMessage.

Kings of Pool

Closed source. Mobile-first with an AR mode that projects a life-size table onto a real surface, plus customizable felt/decals within it.

Cue Club 2

Closed source, desktop. The genre generalist: 8-ball, 9-ball, killer, speed pool and snooker in one game, venue-unlock progression, and a physics-toy "slam" mode.

02Where this points next

The eight gaps below are the ones every competitor above has that we don't, ranked by how much they'd change what the game can do versus how much they'd cost to build. Green means it has shipped.

✓

A second game mode — 9-ball

Shipped. Ten-ball diamond rack, lowest-ball-first, the cushion rule, 9-on-a-legal-shot wins (combinations included), 9 re-spotted on a foul, ball in hand after any foul. It reused the whole physics/rendering/networking stack — this was a new rules.js variant and a smaller rack, exactly as scoped. Available in practice, private tables and quick play; unranked until the ranking tables know which game a result came from.

Shipped
✓

Replay links

Shipped. Every shot is recorded as it plays — by the host from its simulation, by guests and spectators from the snapshots they receive — and Copy replay link packs it into a URL. A full 16-ball break is about 1,000 characters. Anyone can open the link and watch it (at full or quarter speed), with sound. It records positions rather than re-simulating inputs, on purpose: two JavaScript engines needn't agree to the last bit on sin, cos or pow, and billiards amplifies a last-bit difference into a different shot.

Shipped
✓

Trick-shot / practice drill library

Shipped. Eleven one-shot drills on fixed tables, from a straight-in pot to draw, follow, stop, bank, combination and kick shots, with a level, a tip, a Show me that dials in the aim, power and spin, and progress kept in the browser. A solve the hint aimed completes the drill but sets no record. Every drill is proven solvable: a solver searched the real physics for each one, and the tests run its hint through the game again, so a physics change that breaks a drill fails the build. It also caught that the break tip was wrong (full power with no spin scratches every time).

Shipped
✓

Achievements / trophies

Shipped. Twenty-seven trophies in seven groups (racks, nine-ball, skill, opponents, practice, social, league), each with a progress bar toward its target, a toast when one unlocks and a trophy case in the header. Stats are derived from what happened on the table, so host and guest agree, and kept per device. Building it also found and fixed a real bug: a shot that ended before the host's first snapshot was invisible to a guest.

Shipped
5

Native mobile app wrapper

Four of the five competitors are mobile-native or mobile-first. The PWA already installs and works offline for practice; wrapping it (Capacitor/Tauri-style) for app-store presence is packaging, not new engineering — but it's real ongoing work (store review, push notifications, update cadence).

Decision: not planned. The installable PWA is the mobile app.

Not planned
6

Friends list / social graph

Right now every match is a room code or matchmaking — there's no way to find "that person I played last week" again on purpose. Miniclip's Messenger/iMessage integration is the extreme version; even a simple friends list closes most of the gap.

Decision: deferred. Not needed yet; revisit when there are enough regulars to want it.

Deferred
7

Tournament mode

Bracket play with escalating stakes is a Miniclip/Cue Club 2 staple and would put our race-to-N match format and Elo system to more use. Needs real design work (bracket state, byes, spectator flow for eliminated players) beyond a single PR.

Lower priority
✓

Physics-constant inspector

Shipped, read-only. TABLE → How the physics works lists every constant the simulation runs on (friction, restitution, spin decay, rest threshold, the fixed step), what each does, the power-to-speed curve in game units and m/s, and ball and pocket size per table. Values are read live from physics.js, and a test fails if a constant is added there without being described, so the panel cannot drift. It is deliberately not tweakable: the same numbers drive every game, so every player sees the same shot.

Shipped

03What none of them have

Catching up on the list above only makes the game as good as the field. These are things none of the five competitors is known to offer, chosen because the game already has the parts to build them: pure rules, an AI that can play out a shot, replays as links, drills that are proven solvable, and a host-authoritative simulation.

✓

Daily shot

Shipped. One fixed table a day, the same for everyone, that a search of the real physics has shown can be solved. Difficulty rises through the week, the result is a shareable line, and there is no account or server, so it works offline. Two trophies hang off it.

Shipped
10

Shot coach

After a shot, show the shot the AI would have played from the same table, and how likely yours was to work. The AI already plays every candidate out on a copy of the table to screen out fouls; the same machinery can grade a human shot. Nobody else explains why a shot was good or bad.

High impact
11

Hot-seat: two players, one device

Pass the phone. No room code, no network, no account: the rules and physics already run without one. Cheap, and the way most people first play pool with a friend.

Medium impact
12

Bank pool and one-pocket

Two more game modes that are mostly rules: every pot must be a bank (the game already tracks which balls touched a cushion), or each player owns a single pocket. Written as pure functions like nine-ball, so the AI can play them too.

Medium impact
13

Cosmetics earned, not bought

New cues, felts and ball sets unlocked by trophies. The game has no ads and no in-app purchases, which is a selling point the mobile leaders cannot copy; this gives the trophies something to be for.

Medium impact
14

Replay director

The replay is a recording of positions, so it can be played from any camera: scrub, slow-mo through the contact, orbit the cue ball. A shared shot becomes a clip worth sending.

Lower priority
15

Made for everyone to see

Colour-blind-safe ball patterns, spoken shot commentary for screen readers, and larger touch targets. Every competitor relies on colour alone to tell solids from stripes.

Lower priority
